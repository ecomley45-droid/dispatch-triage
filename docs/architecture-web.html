Dispatch · System Map

How the whole app wires together

One field-service platform, drawn as a web: the two tiers that serve it, the roles that gate it, the records that hang off every customer, and the flows that move a job from a phone call to a paid invoice.

01 Two tiers, one server, two gates

Three front doors reach one Express API. Every authenticated request has its org_id injected from the verified session — never read from the client — then passes a read gate (can this role see the page?) and a write gate (can it perform this capability?) before the org-scoped store answers.

02 Who can do what

Authorization is one capability catalog shared by server and client (lib/permissions.js). Four presets ship; workspaces can also define custom roles. A capability is a write/action; page visibility is a separate read gate. The Technician is restricted — it only ever sees its own assigned records.

03 The data web · customer to cash

Everything a service business does hangs off the customer. Sites and assets describe where and what; work orders are the unit of field work; invoices bill them. Tickets and maintenance plans feed back into the same spine.

04 The data web · tenancy & field operations

The other half of the model: the org that owns everything, the people and structure inside it, and the project/dispatch side of field work that runs in parallel to the customer spine.

05 A work order's life

The core state machine. A job enters from three doors, moves through the field, gets a manager's sign-off, and ends as an invoice. Two states fire side effects automatically.

06 The customer's side of the wall

Customers never sign in. A single rotating token grants a scoped view of just their own records, plus four actions that write straight back into the staff-side spine.

07 The operator's console

Above every workspace sits the platform tier. A super-admin never belongs to a workspace — they operate across all of them from /super-admin.
