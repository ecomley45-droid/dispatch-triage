Where Dispatch stands

Dispatch doesn't try to out-feature ServiceTitan. It competes on a different axis: cover the operational core cleanly, stay portable and affordable, and be the only one on the list you can put your own name on.

Dispatch's edge

  • White-label & multi-tenant. The only platform here you can resell to other operators under your own brand — no competitor offers this at all.
  • Projects & punch lists. A construction-crossover the pure field-service tools skip — useful for larger, multi-visit jobs.
  • Portal + ticketing. Customers view jobs, request work, pay, and hold a threaded support conversation — most rivals stop at a read-only portal.
  • Portability & transparency. One flat price, one-click full JSON export, no lock-in — against an industry known for quotes-only pricing and sticky data.
  • Modern baseline. Custom roles, regions, an AI assistant, and offline all included, not gated behind the top tier.

Where it's behind

  • No quotes / estimates. The biggest gap — every competitor sells before it invoices. Dispatch jumps straight to invoicing.
  • No QuickBooks. Sage Intacct is covered, but QuickBooks is the SMB accounting standard the others all integrate.
  • No marketing / reviews / financing. Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan win growth-minded shops on these.
  • Web app, not native. The PWA installs and works offline, but there's no App Store / Play Store presence.
  • Young ecosystem. Thin integrations marketplace, no public booking widget, and none of the incumbents' scale, support org, or track record.

The one-line read

Against the incumbents, Dispatch lands as a lean, portable, white-label alternative that nails the customer→work-order→invoice→payment loop and adds project management on top — ideal for an operator (or a reseller) who wants ServiceTitan-style structure without the per-tech pricing, the lock-in, or the six-week onboarding.

To go toe-to-toe on the SMB shortlist with Jobber and Housecall Pro, the priority additions are clear: quotes/estimates, QuickBooks sync, and a public booking widget — the three rows above where it currently shows a dash the others don't.
